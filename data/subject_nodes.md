Topic 2: Young's Double Slit Experiment (YDSE)
Young's Double Slit Experiment demonstrates the wave nature of light through the phenomenon of interference. In this experiment, monochromatic light passes through two closely spaced narrow slits, which act as coherent sources. The light waves overlap on a screen and produce alternate bright and dark fringes.

Topic 3: Diffraction of Light
Diffraction is the bending or spreading of light waves when they pass through a narrow opening or around an obstacle whose dimensions are comparable to the wavelength of light.

It demonstrates the wave nature of light.

Types of Diffraction:

1. Fresnel Diffraction: Occurs when the source of light and screen are at finite distances from the obstacle or aperture. The incident wavefronts are spherical or curved.

2. Fraunhofer Diffraction: Occurs when the source and screen are effectively at infinite distances. The incident and diffracted wavefronts are approximately plane waves.

Topic 4: Polarization of Light
Polarization is the phenomenon in which vibrations of light waves are restricted to a particular direction perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

It proves that light is a transverse wave.

Types of Polarization:

Linear Polarization: The electric field vibrates in one fixed direction.

Circular Polarization: The electric field rotates with constant magnitude, tracing a circle.

Elliptical Polarization: The electric field traces an elliptical path.

Topic 8: Applications of Wave Optics
Wave optics has many important applications in science, engineering, and modern technology.

Optical Coatings: Used to reduce reflection in lenses and optical instruments.

Holography: Uses interference and diffraction to record and reconstruct three-dimensional images.

Spectroscopy: Used to analyse wavelengths and identify different materials.

Polarized Sunglasses: Reduce glare caused by reflected light.

Optical Communication: Helps in understanding the transmission of light signals.

Interferometers: Used to measure very small distances and changes in length.

Microscopes and Telescopes: Wave optics helps improve the design and performance of optical instruments.
